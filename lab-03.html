Environmental sensing and time-varying signal measurement.

  2. Characterize a photoresistor under different lighting conditions.
  3. Use a photoresistor in a voltage-divider circuit.
  4. Use DC and AC coupling appropriately on an oscilloscope.

02

Necessary Concepts

Review these ideas before starting the practical work.

Sensors and Transduction

A sensor responds to a physical condition such as light or sound and produces a corresponding electrical change. This conversion is called transduction. A photoresistor changes resistance with illumination, while a microphone produces a small time-varying electrical response to sound pressure.

Photoresistor and Voltage Divider

A photoresistor normally has a lower resistance in bright light and a higher resistance in darkness. When it is combined with a fixed resistor as a voltage divider, the resistance change becomes a measurable output voltage. The direction of the voltage change depends on whether the photoresistor is placed above or below the output node.

Vout = VSR1 /(RLDR + R1)

DC and AC Coupling

DC coupling displays the complete input signal, including its constant level and time-varying part. AC coupling blocks the constant level and displays only the changing part. AC coupling is useful when a small ripple is hidden on a much larger DC voltage.

Triggering

A trigger tells the oscilloscope when to capture and align a waveform. Edge triggering responds when the signal crosses a selected threshold. A rising-edge trigger responds to a low-to-high crossing; a falling-edge trigger responds to a high-to-low crossing. In Auto mode the display continues updating even if no valid event occurs. In Normal mode it updates only after the chosen event is detected.

Safety: Connect every oscilloscope probe ground clip only to the circuit ground. Observe microphone and phototransistor polarity. Disable the source output before changing the circuit.

Task 1: Characterize the Photoresistor

Measure how the photoresistor responds to three different lighting conditions.

  1. Disconnect the photoresistor from all powered circuits.
  2. Set the multimeter to resistance measurement.
  3. Measure the resistance in ambient light.
  4. Cover the sensing surface and repeat the measurement.
  5. Illuminate the sensing surface with a bright nearby light and repeat.
  6. State the relationship between illumination and resistance.

Task 4: Capture a Sound Event

Use an electret microphone and edge triggering to capture a short clap or finger snap.

  1. Identify the microphone ground terminal and construct the circuit with correct polarity.
  2. Set the source to a constant 10 V and enable the oscilloscope channel bandwidth limit if available.
  3. Adjust the display so the silent level and the largest sound response are both visible.
  4. Select an edge trigger and place its threshold slightly below the expected peak.
  5. Use Normal mode to capture one clap or snap.
  6. Repeat using rising and falling slopes and compare the captured events.

Task 5: Observe an Infrared Remote Signal

Detect the optical pulses produced by a remote control and measure their timing.

  1. Construct the detector with the phototransistor polarity shown.
  2. Aim the remote directly toward the phototransistor and hold one button.
  3. Start with a wide time view so that several bursts are visible.
  4. Use triggering and cursors to measure the burst duration and pause.
  5. Zoom in and measure the slower pattern inside one burst.
  6. Zoom further to estimate the higher modulation frequency.
  7. Repeat with another remote button and compare the patterns.
