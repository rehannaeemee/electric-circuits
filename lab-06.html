Gamepad Assembly and Testing

Lab Overview

Assemble a gamepad that connects pushbuttons and a joystick to a computer.

Goal

Build an interface circuit that allows a player to control a computer game. Use an Arduino-Pro-Micro to read five pushbuttons and the Right Joystick, then send their inputs to the computer over USB.

In this lab, you will build a gamepad for Counter Strike. The pushbuttons can be assigned to actions such as jumping and shooting, while the joystick provides directional inputs. You will apply the pushbutton and joystick measurements practised in the earlier labs.

Learning Outcomes

  1. Assemble a circuit from a schematic and check its connections.
  2. Mount the microcontroller in removable female header connectors.
  3. Upload the supplied firmware and test the electrical inputs.
  4. Configure the gamepad controls and demonstrate their operation in a game.

Equipment and Components

  • Five external pushbuttons, labelled S1–S5 in the circuit diagram
  • One Right Joystick module, labelled KY-032, including its built-in SW push switch
  • Arduino-Pro-Micro microcontroller and a USB data cable
  • Veroboard, female header connectors, and male/female jumper wires
  • Digital multimeter and suitable soldering equipment
  • Computer with the programming software, supplied firmware and libraries, and the game

Important Messages

  • Follow the circuit diagram in Step 1: use S1–S5 and the Right Joystick only. Its SW pin provides an additional push-switch input.
  • Do not solder the microcontroller directly to the Veroboard. Solder female header connectors to the Veroboard and insert the microcontroller into them.
  • Disconnect USB power before changing wiring or soldering. Check for shorts and poor solder joints before reconnecting.
  • The joystick supply pin retains its printed +5V label in the diagram and is connected to the microcontroller VCC. Confirm the voltage of the actual board before powering the circuit.
  • The reference readings in Step 3 correspond to the 3.3 V setup. Use the measured VCC when interpreting your readings.
02

Necessary Concepts

Understand the signals before building and testing the gamepad.

Pushbutton Inputs

Each external pushbutton connects its microcontroller input to GND when pressed. With the input pull-up enabled in the firmware, the released input is near VCC and the pressed input is near 0 V. The microcontroller uses these two states to identify a button press.

Joystick Inputs

The joystick contains two potentiometers. VRx and VRy provide position-dependent voltages to analog inputs A0 and A1. At the released centre position, each voltage is normally close to half the supply voltage. The joystick’s built-in push switch is connected through SW to digital input D2.

Microcontroller and USB Interface

The Arduino-Pro-Micro reads the button states and joystick positions. The supplied firmware sends these inputs to the computer over USB so that the configured controls can produce actions in the game.

Firmware and Control Mapping

Uploading firmware, also called burning the code, places the supplied program in the microcontroller. Configuring the gamepad is a separate step: it assigns a game action to each available control. The firmware pin assignments must match the circuit diagram.

Voltage Reference

Pro Micro boards are available in 3.3 V and 5 V versions. Identify the board used in the lab and measure VCC relative to GND. A released button input should be near that voltage, and the joystick centre should be near half of it. The nominal readings of 3.3 V and 1.6 V below apply to the 3.3 V setup.

Board reference: SparkFun Pro Micro hardware overview. Pushbutton reference: Arduino digital input pull-up example.

Practical Tasks and Worksheets

Complete the five steps in sequence and record the measurements and observations.

Step 1 — Circuit Patching

Assemble five external pushbuttons and the Right Joystick according to the circuit diagram.

  1. Keep the circuit disconnected from the computer while assembling it.
  2. Plan the positions of the pushbuttons and joystick on the Veroboard so that they are comfortable to operate.
  3. Solder female header connectors to the Veroboard for the Arduino-Pro-Micro. Insert the microcontroller into the headers rather than soldering it directly to the Veroboard.
  4. Connect S1–S5 to digital pins D3–D7 respectively, with the other terminal of each pushbutton connected to GND.
  5. Connect the Right Joystick VRx to A0, VRy to A1 and SW to D2. Connect its supply pin, labelled +5V, to VCC and its GND pin to the common GND.
  6. Use the multimeter continuity function on the unpowered circuit to check the connections. Inspect for solder bridges, loose joints and unintended shorts between VCC and GND.

Step 3 — Hardware Testing

Measure the electrical inputs before configuring the game.

  1. Connect the assembled gamepad to the computer with the USB cable and confirm that the microcontroller is powered.
  2. Set the multimeter to DC voltage mode. Connect its black lead to the common GND and measure VCC using the red lead.
  3. Measure the voltage at each pushbutton signal terminal when the button is released and when it is pressed. Include the joystick SW input.
  4. Release the joystick to its centre position. Measure VRx and VRy relative to GND.
  5. Compare the measurements with the expected levels. If a reading is inconsistent, disconnect USB power and check the connections and soldering before retesting.

Reference readings for the 3.3 V setup: a released button input is approximately 3.3 V, a pressed input is near 0 V, and the joystick centre voltage is approximately 1.6 V. For another VCC, expect the released input near VCC and each centre output near VCC/2.

Step 5 — Testing the Gamepad

Demonstrate that the assembled circuit controls the game.

  1. Install and open Counter Strike using the game download supplied for the lab.
  2. Load the saved gamepad configuration and begin a test session.
  3. Test the joystick in all four directions and test each configured button, including the jumping and shooting controls.
  4. If an action is missing or assigned incorrectly, check the electrical readings and control mapping, then repeat the test.
  5. Capture the gamepad and game screen in working condition for the report and demonstration video.

Describe the test results and any remaining issues with the controls.
