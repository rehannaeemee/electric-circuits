Lab 05

Joystick Measurements and Oscilloscope XY Mode

Explore a dual-axis joystick as two voltage dividers. Measure its X and Y outputs, connect both outputs to an oscilloscope, and use XY mode to draw a shape on the screen.

Lab Overview

Investigate how joystick movement becomes two electrical signals.

Learning Outcomes

  1. Identify the power, ground and two analog output pins of a dual-axis joystick.
  2. Explain the operation of the joystick using two potentiometer voltage dividers.
  3. Measure output voltages at the centre and the four end positions.
  4. Relate changes in the two voltages to motion on an oscilloscope XY display.
  5. Use display persistence to trace a shape and draw the letter F.

Equipment

HW-504 dual-axis joystick module, breadboard, male–female jumper wires, regulated DC power supply, digital multimeter, and a two-channel oscilloscope with two probes.

Important Messages

  • For a module whose supply pin is labelled +5V, use a regulated 5 V supply. Confirm the actual module pin labels before connecting power.
  • Switch off the supply while changing connections. Connect supply negative, joystick GND and both oscilloscope ground clips to the same ground point.
  • Connect probe tips to the output pins. Connect both probe ground clips only to GND.
  • Keep the module orientation fixed throughout the measurements. Leave the SW pin unconnected in this lab.
02

Necessary Concepts

Connect potentiometer operation with voltage measurements and screen position.

Two Potentiometers in One Joystick

A dual-axis joystick contains two potentiometers. Moving the stick along one axis changes one potentiometer’s wiper position; moving it along the other axis changes the second. Each potentiometer is connected between the supply and ground, and its wiper provides an analog voltage.

For an unloaded divider, the output voltage equals the supply voltage multiplied by the resistance from the wiper to ground, divided by the total potentiometer resistance. The output therefore varies with stick position. At the spring-return centre it is usually near half the supply, but the measured value need not be exact.

Joystick Pins

Pin label Function
GND Common ground reference
+5V / VCC Supply input; follow the label and rating of the actual module
VRx Wiper output of the X-axis potentiometer
VRy Wiper output of the Y-axis potentiometer
SW Push switch output; unused in this lab

Read the printed labels on the board rather than assuming a pin order from a photograph. Output polarity depends on module construction and orientation; determine from your measurements which physical movement increases each voltage.

Oscilloscope XY Mode

In XY mode, one input controls horizontal position and the other controls vertical position. For the oscilloscope used here, connect CH1 to VRx for X and CH2 to VRy for Y; confirm the X and Y channel assignment on the available instrument. A stationary stick produces a stationary point. Changing one output moves the point along one screen axis; changing both can produce a diagonal or curved trace.

Use DC coupling on both channels to retain the position information. Infinite persistence keeps earlier points visible, allowing the movement path to remain on the display. The screen records voltage coordinates; the displayed direction may differ from the physical stick direction.

Practical Tasks and Worksheets

Complete the activities in order and record your own measurements.

Task 1: Connect the Joystick and Identify Its Positions

Prepare the module for voltage measurements.

  1. Identify GND, the supply input, VRx and VRy on the actual joystick module.
  2. With the supply switched off, connect GND to supply negative and the +5V pin to the positive output using the breadboard and male–female jumpers.
  3. Set the supply to 5 V for the +5V-labelled module. Have the wiring checked, then turn on the supply.
  4. Measure the voltage between the supply input and GND using the multimeter in DC voltage mode.
  5. Keep the pin-header side facing the same direction throughout the lab. Label the released centre as position 0, the two horizontal ends as 1 and 2, and the two vertical ends as 3 and 4. Use the instructor’s position diagram when provided.

Task 2: Measure the Joystick Output Voltages

Measure each output relative to the same ground reference.

Part A: X Output

  1. Connect the multimeter black lead to GND and the red lead to VRx. Select DC voltage measurement.
  2. Release the joystick to position 0 and record the centre voltage.
  3. Move the stick to position 2, hold it there and record the voltage.
  4. Move the stick to position 1, hold it there and record the voltage. Release it to the centre.

Part B: Y Output

  1. Move the red lead from VRx to VRy. Keep the black lead connected to GND.
  2. Record the voltage at position 0, then at positions 3 and 4, holding the stick steady at each end.

Task 3: Observe Joystick Movement in XY Mode

Compare movement on the display with the voltage readings from Task 2.

  1. Switch off the supply before attaching the oscilloscope probes.
  2. Connect the CH1 probe tip to VRx and the CH2 probe tip to VRy. Connect both ground clips to joystick GND.
  3. Turn on the supply and oscilloscope. Enable both channels, use DC coupling and match each channel’s probe setting to the probe attenuation.
  4. Open the Display menu and select Type: Vector, Format: XY and Persist: Infinite on the lab oscilloscope. On another model, use the equivalent XY and persistence settings.
  5. Adjust both voltage scales and position controls so the centre point and full movement range remain visible.
  6. Starting at position 0, move to position 2 and observe the direction of the trace. Compare it with the change in VRx measured in Task 2.
  7. Clear the previous trace, return to position 0, and repeat for positions 1, 3 and 4. Record the screen direction for each movement.
  8. On the oscilloscope shown in the source instructions, press F3 once to refresh the display. Use the corresponding clear or refresh control if the available model differs.
  9. Challenge: Match the direction of movement of joystick with that of the trace on the scope. Discuss the solution with instructor.

Task 4: Draw the Letter F

Control both voltages to form a recognisable letter on the XY display.

  1. Keep XY mode and infinite persistence enabled. Clear the existing display.
  2. Use the directions identified in Task 3 to trace a vertical stroke, a top horizontal stroke and a shorter middle horizontal stroke.
  3. Retrace existing strokes when moving between them to limit unwanted connecting lines.
  4. Repeat until the letter F is recognisable. Freeze the display and take a photograph.

Describe the stick movements used to draw F and explain why persistence is needed.

04

Review

Use your observations to explain joystick operation.

1. Why does a dual-axis joystick require two potentiometers?

2. Why are the output voltages usually near half the supply when the stick is released?

3. What controls horizontal and vertical position in XY mode?

4. What would change on the display if the CH1 and CH2 signal connections were exchanged?

5. What happens to a drawn shape when persistence is cleared?

  • Completed XY movement table and written explanations.
  • Photographs of the XY trace and the letter F.
